# Nighttime Coughing: Common Causes and When to Seek Care

## Learn why coughing often intensifies at night, how airway physiology changes when lying flat, and what red flags warrant clinical attention.

By Pulmonary and Respiratory Health Advisers

Coughing is an essential protective physiological reflex that clears your respiratory tract of irritants, foreign particles, and excess secretions. However, when coughing episodes occur repeatedly throughout the night, they fragment sleep stages, dry out vocal cords, and disrupt bed partners.

Understanding why nocturnal coughing occurs can help you take practical steps to alleviate throat irritation and identify when medical evaluation is necessary.

---

## Why Coughing Gets Worse When Lying Down

### 1. Gravity and Postnasal Drip
During the day, when you are standing or sitting, gravity naturally draws nasal secretions down your esophagus. When you lie horizontal at night, mucus from allergies, sinus infections, or colds pools at the back of the pharynx, irritating cough receptors.

### 2. Gastroesophageal Reflux (GERD / LPR)
Lying flat removes the gravitational barrier that keeps stomach acid inside the stomach. Acid and pepsin can creep up the esophagus into the larynx and pharynx (silent reflux or LPR), triggering airway spasm and chronic dry hacking coughs without classic heartburn symptoms.

### 3. Dry, Cold Bedroom Air
Inhaling dry air (especially in winter heating or strong air conditioning) dries out the mucosal lining of the upper airway, triggering hypersensitivity in cough receptors.

### 4. Cough-Variant Asthma
In individuals with asthma, nocturnal circadian dips in epinephrine and cortisol combined with airway cooling lead to bronchoconstriction, often manifesting primarily as a persistent dry night cough.

## Practical Home Remedies for Nocturnal Coughing

### 1. Elevate Your Head and Torso
Prop the head of your bed up by **10–15 cm (4–6 inches)** using bed risers or a wedge pillow. Propping with multiple regular pillows may bend your neck, constricting airways; use a sloped wedge to elevate your entire upper torso.

### 2. Optimize Bedroom Humidity
Keep indoor relative humidity between **40% and 55%**. Use a cool-mist ultrasonic humidifier in dry climates or during heating season to keep respiratory mucous membranes moist. Clean the humidifier regularly to prevent mold buildup.

### 3. Saline Nasal Rinse
Perform a gentle saline nasal irrigation (Neti pot or sterile saline spray) 30 minutes before bed to wash away allergens, pollen, and accumulated mucus from nasal passages.

### 4. Evening Meal Timing for Acid Reflux
Avoid eating within **3 hours of lying down**, and limit acidic, spicy, high-fat foods, chocolate, and caffeine in the evening to reduce reflux triggers.

---

## Red Flags: When to See a Physician Immediately

While occasional coughing from a mild viral cold is common, you should seek prompt medical evaluation if your nighttime coughing is accompanied by any of the following symptoms:

- **Difficulty breathing, wheezing, or stridor (high-pitched breathing sounds)**
- **Chest pain or tightness**
- **Coughing up blood or rust-colored phlegm**
- **Unexplained fever, chills, or night sweats**
- **Cough lasting longer than 3 to 4 weeks (chronic cough)**
- **Waking up choking, gasping, or with persistent hoarseness**

---

## Summary Key Takeaways

- **Position**: Elevate head/torso with a wedge pillow to combat postnasal drip and reflux.
- **Air Quality**: Maintain 40%–55% bedroom humidity with clean humidification.
- **Hydration**: Drink warm, non-caffeinated fluids earlier in the evening.
- **Seek Care**: Consult a doctor if cough lasts over 3 weeks, produces blood, or impairs breathing.
